title: Suspicious calls and text messages
category: Security
tags: [vishing, smishing, scams]
updated: 2025-07-08
---
Scammers call and text Contoso Health staff while pretending to be the service desk, a vendor, or an executive, so hang up and call back on a number you already know before you share anything.

Phone scams are called vishing, and text message scams are called smishing. Caller ID and text sender names can be faked, so a call that shows 555-0100, or a text signed with an executive's name, proves nothing.

The Contoso Health service desk will never:
1. Ask for your password, or for an MFA code you did not request.
2. Ask you to approve a Microsoft Authenticator prompt you did not start, or read you a number to type into Authenticator. A caller who does that is trying to sign in as you.
3. Call you out of the blue and ask you to install remote control software.
4. Ask you to buy gift cards or move money.

When you call the service desk yourself, it may text a one-time code to the phone number on file and ask you to read it back. That is the only time to read a code to anyone. If someone calls you and asks for a code, it is a scam.

If you get a suspicious call:
1. Do not confirm your username, employee ID, or personal details.
2. Hang up, then call the service desk at extension 4357 (H E L P) or 555-0100. Use the number from the directory, never one the caller gives you.
3. Tell the service desk what the caller wanted and the number that called you.

If you get a suspicious text:
1. Do not tap links or reply, not even to say STOP.
2. Take a screenshot, email it to phishing@contoso-health.example, then delete the text.

If you already shared your password or approved a prompt, reset your password right away at passwordreset.contoso-health.example. Then call the service desk so a technician can check your account and sign out any session the scammer opened.

Still stuck? Call the service desk at extension 4357 (H E L P) or 555-0100 and say you are reporting a scam.
