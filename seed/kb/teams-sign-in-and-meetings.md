Most Teams sign-in problems clear up after you quit and reset Teams, and most meeting join problems clear up when you join from the Join button in the invitation.

If Teams will not sign you in:

1. Sign in with your full work email address, not just your username.
2. Approve the Microsoft Authenticator request by typing the number shown on your screen.
3. If Teams keeps returning to the sign-in screen, quit it completely. On Windows, right-click the Teams icon near the clock and select Quit. On a Mac, select Teams in the menu bar, then Quit.
4. Reset Teams on Windows by opening Settings, then Apps, then Installed apps. Find Microsoft Teams, select Advanced options, then Reset. On a Mac, reinstall Teams from Self Service.
5. Open Teams and sign in again.

If your account is locked, or Teams rejects a password you just changed, fix that first. See the article on account lockouts.

If you cannot join a meeting:

1. Use the Join button in the meeting invitation in Outlook or the Teams calendar, not a link someone pasted in a chat.
2. If Teams says you are waiting in the lobby, the organizer has to admit you. Send them a chat message.
3. For meetings hosted by another organization, select Continue on this browser if the app shows an error, and enter your name.
4. If the app will not work at all, join by phone using the dial-in number and conference ID in the invitation.

Teams on your phone uses the same work account, so the same sign-in steps apply.
