If an email looks suspicious, report it with the Report Phishing button in Outlook, or forward it as an attachment to phishing@contoso-health.example.

Phishing emails try to get you to click a link, open a file, or hand over information. Watch for pressure to act fast, such as a warning that your account or paycheck will be stopped. Watch for a familiar sender name with an email address that does not match, a sign-in link that does not lead to a contoso-health.example address, or an unexpected invoice, attachment, or shared file. Any request for your password, an MFA code, gift cards, or a change to your direct deposit is a warning sign.

To report a suspicious email:
1. Do not click links, open attachments, or reply.
2. In Outlook, select the message and choose Report Phishing. The button is on the ribbon in the Outlook desktop app, and in the message menu in Outlook on the web and the Outlook mobile app. The message goes to the security team and is removed from your inbox.
3. If your mail app has no Report Phishing button, forward the message as an attachment to phishing@contoso-health.example. In Outlook, this is under More actions, then Forward as attachment. A normal forward drops details the security team needs.
4. Do not forward the message to coworkers to ask whether it is real.

If you already clicked a link and typed your password, reset it right away at passwordreset.contoso-health.example, then call the service desk. If you opened an attachment, or approved a Microsoft Authenticator prompt you did not start, call the service desk immediately and say you are reporting a security incident.

Reporting is never a waste of time, even when a message turns out to be safe. Each report helps the security team block the same message for everyone else.

The service desk never asks for your password or for an MFA code you did not request.

Still stuck? Call the service desk at extension 4357 (H E L P) or 555-0100.
