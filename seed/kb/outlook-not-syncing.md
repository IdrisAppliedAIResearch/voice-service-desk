If Outlook stops sending or receiving email, work through these checks in order, because the first three fix most problems.

1. Check that Outlook is online. If the status bar at the bottom of classic Outlook says Working Offline or Disconnected, open the Send / Receive tab and select Work Offline to turn it off.
2. Check your network. If websites and Teams do not load either, fix the network connection first.
3. Restart Outlook. Close it completely, wait a few seconds, and open it again. If it asks you to sign in, use your work email address.
4. Look in your Outbox. One message with a large attachment can hold up everything behind it. Move it to Drafts, then share the file as a OneDrive link instead. Attachments over 25 MB are rejected.
5. Check your mailbox space. If Outlook says your mailbox is full, empty Deleted Items and move older messages to an archive folder.
6. Compare with Outlook on the web. Sign in from a web browser. If new mail appears there but not in the app, the problem is the Outlook app on your computer.

If only the app is affected, create a new Outlook profile. Close Outlook, open Control Panel, select Mail, then Show Profiles, and select Add. Set Always use this profile to the new one, and open Outlook. A large mailbox can take an hour or more to download again.

If you changed your password recently, Outlook may keep asking for it until you close and reopen it. Update the password in your phone's email app at the same time, or it can lock your account.
