Every service desk ticket gets a priority from P1 to P4, based on how many people are affected and whether patient care is involved, and the priority sets how fast the service desk responds.

P1, critical: patient care is affected, or many users are down with no workaround. Examples are a nursing unit that cannot open the electronic health record, or a network outage across a whole building. The response target is 15 minutes. P1 issues are taken by phone only, at extension 4357 (H E L P) or 555-0100. Never report a P1 by email, chat, or the portal.

P2, high: a department or a critical system is impaired, but patient care can continue. Examples are a billing team that cannot reach its shared drive, or a scheduling system that is slow for an entire clinic. The response target is 1 hour.

P3, medium: one person is affected and a workaround exists. Examples are a docking station that stopped working while the laptop still works on its own, or Outlook search that is not finding messages. The response target is 1 business day.

P4, low: requests and questions. Examples are a software request, access to a shared drive folder, or a how-to question. The response target is 3 business days.

These targets are the service level agreement, or SLA, for how quickly a technician starts work on your ticket. They are not a promise that the problem is fixed in that time, but the technician keeps you updated until it is resolved.

How priority is set:
The service desk sets the priority when the ticket is opened, using these definitions. To get it right the first time, say how many people are affected, whether patients are involved, and whether you have a workaround.

If things change, for example more people are affected or the workaround stops working, call the service desk with your ticket number, ask for the shift lead, and ask for the priority to be reviewed.

Still stuck? Call the service desk at extension 4357 (H E L P) or 555-0100 and have your ticket number ready.
